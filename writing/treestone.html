Poetry

Treestone

Originally published in Möbius: The Poetry Magazine — 2000.

I found a piece of treestone along the riverbank, uncovered several ages past the time it would have sank beneath the muddy shoreline, was covered and compressed by tons of silt and sediment, and there for eons rest. Life continued onward; its trunk, lost to decay, became the food for mosses, is both moss and tree today. Other treestones lost their footing and were slowly ground to sand as they tumbled down the river, settled in some distant land. The air that it exhaled then is the air that fills my lungs. Separated by ten thousand years, I know the songs it sung. They echo through the treetops when the wind rattles the leaves and whisper in the meadows when there’s just a hint of breeze. One piece still holds its former shape, but all have shifted phase, to earth, to wind, to water, who knows how many ways? The tree that was… is different; it is gone but still remains… a drop, part of the ocean, of ebb and flow and change.
