Poetry

The Chase

Originally published in Möbius: The Poetry Magazine — 1999.

They ran; I chased them. Simple, really; I didn’t even think about the choice— I was like a dog chasing a rabbit, except these rabbits had teeth, and claws, if they knew how to use them. There were three of them, dark-clothed and tall. I couldn’t make out much else in the post-midnight blackness, lit only by the headlights of my squad. I called in my situation and location, as we ran across backyards and alleys on the portable I’d pulled from the dash. Backup was rolling but was too far out to help. This would be a solo hunt. I left the flashlight off— it would give more information than it gained— and ran on. I lost sight of them, knew they could be anywhere. The possibility of ambush nagged at the back of my brain, buried under layers of sensory input. I sifted unconsciously for recognizable shapes, and sounds: shadows within shadows, defined more by what they hide than by what they show; an absence of sound where sound should be; changes in acoustics as you move past objects, or they move past you; things so subtle they may register only subliminally on your ever probing mind. Looking for movement, listening for motion, hunting skills performed out of habit and instinct… They had the advantage— there were, after all, three of them, all running on adrenaline, and they knew where they were going. If they turned… the outcome would be in question, especially if they caught me off guard. I was a shadow hunter, forcing myself beyond superstition and whispered fears. Dogs were barking. I headed for the one most excited, he snapping and straining against his leash. His blood was up and he wanted to join the chase, to rush blindly after the quarry. I knew the feeling, the exhilaration of the chase, how it tempts you to release the animal within, held in check only by your will, juxtaposed against the need to push forward, past the whispered fears. Two sides of a coin, held together by the metal between. I raised the three, thanks to the dog, and we ran once more, their fear driving them, my animal pulling me, alive with the thrill of the chase.
