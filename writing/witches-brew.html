Witches Brew

Independently published, 2026.

By fire light, deep in the night, The witches cook their brew, In cauldron black, they mix their snack, Stirred by a creepy crew. The goblin band, in shadows stand, Their eyes all glowing red, They stomp their feet, and scream “Let’s eat!” They're wanting to be fed. The trolls arrive, they number five, And they're a rugged lot, They're large and tough, and kind of rough, They want what's in the pot. The dragon queen, almost unseen, Comes out from hidden cave, She looks around, without a sound, For persons strong and brave. A little boy, his name was Roy, She picked from off the earth, She set him down, upon the ground, In all that monster mirth. He showed no fear, nor shed a tear, As he looked from left to right, He had to stay, can't run away, And he'd never start a fight. He stood his ground, ’til strength he found, To ask, “What’s in the pot?” The witches grinned, the trolls looked in, The goblins though did naught. “It’s Witches Brew, a tasty stew, We make about the best, Not for the meek, come have a peek, You are our honored guest.” So Roy stepped near, no hint of fear, Till by the pot he stood, Tasted the Brew, said as he chewed, “Hey, this is pretty good.” Then howls and hoops, sprang from the troops, A cheer for Halloween, Soon boy and beast, joined in the feast, Made sure their plates were clean. They sang and danced, ’round fire pranced, Till night had passed to day, Then Roy went home, his dreams to roam, The monsters slipped away. But they'll be back, for midnight snack, Next time it might be you, Who dragon queen, takes there unseen, To have some Witches Brew.

About Witches Brew

Written for my children roughly thirty years ago, this deliberately sing-song children's poem was recently illustrated for my grandchildren and became the basis for the published children's book Witches Brew.
