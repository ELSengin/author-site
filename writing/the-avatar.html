Poetry

The Avatar

Originally published in The Leading Edge, Issue 39.

The headless stepped from 'hind the rock to deal his deadly blow, but
Erick The Unready was prepared to meet his foe.
Our hero drew his magic bow and aimed it down a notch,
and felled the hapless headless with an arrow to the crotch.
The fight was far from over, the carnage just begun,
he found himself surrounded with odds at ten to one.
With spell book raised, "Sla Mange," he cried and cast his magic spell,
a lightning bolt crashed from the skies and drove four fiends to hell.
Hot blood was pulsing in his veins, his heart was beating fast;
his halberd flashed and with a gasp the Cyclops breathed his last.
"The odds are better now," he said, "six down and five to go."
The dragons didn't move an inch, the goblins backed up slow.
"Hisss... What are you?" one dragon asked, "Have we not met before?”
"Hisss... Why dost not thou smell of fear?" the other dragon roared.
"Why should I fear you dragon?* I’ve faced your kind before,
and though I don't like braggin', last time they numbered more."
"Hisss… The Avatar!" the first one roared, "This time you'll meet thy fate.”
His nostrils flared in anger, his eyes filled up with hate.
Both worms sped toward our hero, with the goblins close behind;
‘twas clear that their intentions were anything but kind.
Unready charged between them, sword slicing through thick hide.
Flames and lightning answered, dragon claws tore at his side.
The battle raged ferocious as he fought to stay alive;
his blade a streak, a goblin's shriek... no longer faced he five.
"Drepe Gra Fiende," called out Unready; a spell of death was cast,
the old, gray, lightning-dragon succumbed to death at last.
The two remaining goblins fled in terror from the scene.
The lone red fire-dragon circled Erick looking mean.
No strength was left for magic; the rest was up to skill.
The lone Knight faced the dragon as it moved in for the kill.
Our hero: breathing heavy, armor torn, and black with soot,
waited patient for the dragon, moved his weight from foot to foot.
Then lightning fast it forward dashed, like some gigantic dart.
It missed our man, dropped to the sand, a sword thrust through its heart.
"Hisss... I thought I had you Avatar... Unready you appeared."
And with that last, the great beast passed, no more would he be feared.
The fight was done, the battle won, alone our hero stood,
and listened to the silence... was he lucky or just good?
"A bit of both I reckon," said he with thoughtful grin.
And strapping on his sword and shield was ready once again.
Then Erick The Unready walked off slowly to the West.
Though battle torn, his will unworn, he’d passed another test.

* Bill wrote the first 14½ lines. Erick L. Sokn’s continuation begins here.

Finished for Bill by Erick L. Sokn.

About The Avatar

Bill, a friend from my Maytag days, and I used to play adventure role-playing software games together. The Ultima series was one of our favorites. The characters in The Avatar are based on these games.

Bill saw a new game that caught his eye called Eric the Unready. And while we never did play it, he began the poem substituting the spelling of my name for the character’s name. Bill wrote the first 14-½ lines before he passed away.

He read the first part to me in his office just a week before he died and said, “Erick was going to get hurt this time. But that Unready would turn out ready after all.”

I had never met Bill’s wife. When I introduced myself to her at the funeral, she said, “Ahh, the Avatar, that’s how Bill always referred to you.”

I had to finish the poem for him.
