Synergistic Engineering Design · Module 1 of 8

Product Needs & Requirements

This course focuses on defining what the product must accomplish before deciding how to accomplish it. It develops customer and market needs into measurable engineering requirements and an agreed-upon Product Requirements Document that becomes the basis for subsequent design activity.

This course is Module 1 of 8 in the Engineering Product Development Curriculum built on the Synergistic Engineering Design methodology.

Core Topics

  • Basic statement of need
  • Customer and market needs
  • Voice of the Customer, QFD, and CTQ characteristics
  • Competitive benchmarking and product history
  • Engineering requirements and measurable design targets
  • Product Requirements Document development
Detailed Course Outline
  1. Defining the Basic Statement of Need
    • Starting with customer verbatims
    • Identifying what the customer is actually trying to accomplish
    • Separating the underlying need from suggested or assumed solutions
    • Consolidating multiple customer statements into a basic statement of need
    • Identifying customer expectations and critical product needs
  2. Understanding Product and Market Needs
    • Identifying required and desired product features
    • Identifying the functions the customer expects the product to perform
    • Considering aesthetics and other customer expectations
    • Understanding why needs should describe what the customer requires rather than prescribe a solution
    • Incorporating company business objectives and marketing strategy
    • Defining the intended product and application boundaries
  3. Voice of the Customer and Customer-Need Tools
    • Voice of the Customer (VOC)
    • Converting verbatim customer statements into need statements
    • Quality Function Deployment (QFD)
    • Critical to Quality (CTQ) characteristics
    • Establishing relative importance of customer needs
    • Relating customer needs to product characteristics
    • Using the Market Requirements Document (MRD) to summarize market and customer requirements
  4. Competitive and Historical Product Information
    • Benchmarking competitive products
    • Comparing competitive features and performance
    • Identifying opportunities for competitive differentiation
    • Reviewing similar-product history
    • Reviewing test data
    • Reviewing warranty and rework data
    • Reviewing assembly issues
    • Reviewing previously identified failure modes
    • Incorporating lessons learned into new product requirements
  5. Translating Customer Needs into Engineering Requirements
    • Converting customer needs into measurable product characteristics
    • Defining required versus desired performance
    • Establishing measurable engineering targets
    • Identifying operating conditions
    • Defining environmental conditions
    • Defining expected duration and service life
    • Identifying applicable loads and other performance conditions
    • Maintaining traceability between the customer need and the engineering requirement
    • Recognizing tradeoffs among cost, reliability, performance, and features
  6. Product Requirements Document (PRD)
    • Consolidating customer, market, business, and engineering requirements
    • Identifying required features and functions
    • Identifying desired features and functions
    • Establishing measurable product characteristics and targets
    • Documenting operating and environmental conditions
    • Identifying known design risks
    • Identifying potential risk mitigations and planned testing
    • Reviewing and agreeing upon the product requirements
    • Establishing the PRD as the basis for subsequent design activity

Working Engineering Tools

  • QFD / Market Requirements working matrix
  • Product Requirements Document with integrated design-risk, mitigation, and development-testing assessment

Self-Study Engineering Education

The Module I self-study edition expands the instructor-led course with the detail, worked examples, and application context needed to learn the material independently and use it later as an engineering reference. The working QFD/MRD and PRD tools include completed examples so the relationships and calculations can be examined before the tools are adapted to a new project.

Modules I and II are being prepared as the first grouped self-study release.

Engineering Responsibility: Course materials and working tools support engineering analysis and decision-making but do not replace application-specific engineering judgment, verification, testing, and review. Users remain responsible for determining applicability and verifying results for their specific application.
