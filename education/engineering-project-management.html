Engineering Project Management

Turning engineering work into an executable project through scope definition, phase-gated planning, schedules, ownership, financial and resource planning, risk and action tracking, project records, technical reviews, verification gates, change control, production launch, and project closure.

This is a comprehensive engineering project-management course and toolset built around what different levels of projects actually need to control. A focused engineering effort and a major product-development program do not require the same amount of structure, documentation, or tracking. The course examines what must be managed and why, then provides practical tools that can be used directly or adapted to an organization's existing project-management system, terminology, and software.

The framework is secondary to the reasoning. The purpose of a project-management system is to keep technical work visible, establish ownership, control commitments, preserve decisions and project records, manage risk, and carry engineering work through verification, implementation, and closure.

Detailed Course Outline
  1. Project Structure Fundamentals
    • What makes something a project
    • Projects versus ongoing operations
    • Initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and control, and closure
    • Roles and responsibilities
    • Scope, time, cost, quality, and technical performance
  2. Project Authorization & Planning
    • Project charter / Project Work Statement
    • Business and technical objectives
    • Known constraints and readiness
    • Deliverables and accountable owners
    • Critical milestones and approval structure
  3. Requirements & Work Breakdown Structure
    • Connecting market and engineering requirements to project work
    • Phase-gated master project checklist
    • Workstreams, tasks, and subtasks
    • Assigning responsibility, dates, lead time, and completion status
  4. Scheduling & Resources
    • Milestone schedules
    • Gantt planning
    • Dependencies, float, and critical path
    • Resource planning and ownership
    • Matching schedule detail to project complexity
  5. Project Financials & Capital Planning
    • Early engineering estimates versus priced commitments
    • Cost targets and margin
    • BOM, tooling, equipment, labor, samples, agency, and outside-test costs
    • Capital requirements
    • Payback / ROI
    • Authorization gates for major financial commitments
  6. Risk, Actions & Change Control
    • Distinguishing project risk from design risk captured in the PRD and DFMEA
    • Action-item and issue tracking
    • Risk mitigation
    • Escalation and closure with evidence
    • Scope, schedule, and approved-concept changes
  7. Project Records & Status Reporting
    • Matching the tracking system to project complexity
    • Excel-based project tracking
    • Comprehensive OneNote engineering project records
    • Linking technical reviews, calculations, test results, photographs, communications, and decisions
    • Project metrics, milestones, risks, accomplishments, and next actions
    • Adapting the same underlying needs to commercial project-management platforms where used
  8. Technical Reviews & Test Gates
    • Cross-discipline reviews
    • Concept and design reviews
    • Feasibility testing
    • Qualification testing
    • Verification on production-intent hardware
    • Validation and production readiness
  9. DMAIC as a Project Framework
    • Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, and Control mapped to project work
    • Using the framework to support the reasoning rather than replace it
    • Combining technical rigor with schedule, ownership, visibility, and closure
  10. Implementation & Project Closure
    • Production launch and transition planning
    • Final documentation and handoff
    • Objectives versus actual results
    • Schedule and financial performance
    • Achievements, failures, remaining actions, and lessons learned
    • Feeding lessons back into future requirements, designs, and project plans
